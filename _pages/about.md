<div class="scrim-clip" id="about-bio" data-scrim-label="bio" style="--scrim-clip: 13rem;" markdown="1">

Stedman Halliday is a technical generalist building products, media, and experiences in San Francisco. His work has spanned strategy, design, and code in big tech, global agency, and early-stage company settings.

Stedman spent his early career in edtech, first on the founding team for Kadenze building a platform for creative tech education, and later volunteering as a special educator in Thailand to co-design learning aids and a pilot curriculum that grew into the nation's first university course in programming for blind students. He earned a BFA in Graphic Design with a minor in Digital Arts at California Institute of the Arts, where he twice received the AIGA Tomoko Miho Award for design excellence and later taught web development.

At Wunderman Thompson, he designed through the pandemic for Microsoft Education's shift to remote learning, the Windows 11 launch, and Acura's pivot to fully online vehicle sales. At Amazon Alexa, he led design for emerging voice and multimodal technologies and built the design system that unified third-party deployments.

A move to San Francisco pulled him toward health. He helped scale Eat Blueprint, a longevity meal service, from launch to a million-dollar business acquired by Bryan Johnson in three quarters. He then built digital health MVPs with two founding teams before starting Frontier Fitness Center to integrate software and hardware with a brick-and-mortar facility and its offerings. He has also consulted for AI startups, shaping B2B sales agents for 11x, consumer companion design for Auren, and multi-agent orchestration concepts for Random Labs (YC S24).

He is an accomplished community builder, having grown Designers Guild into a global network that supported over 20,000 creatives across 100 countries. When Stedman reflects on a career full of exploration, he is proudest of the work he's done to increase wellness and connection in the world.

</div>
